- Observed trigger and contract: Bounded protocol work phase (P01, P02) implemented, tested, ponytail-self-reviewed, and Opus-5-reviewed before opening a PR, all on `codex/bounded-protocol-p01-p02`. Both cards found a real, previously-untested "unbounded work in one synchronous call" defect: P01's `process_retransmit` could encrypt/queue an entire retained loss list from one NAK; P02's `pop_due_ids` could fire timers for every due session (hundreds, in the acceptance criteria's own words) before any budget applied, even inside the function whose own docstring claims to be bounded. Ponytail self-review found nothing to cut (2 commits, one new production constant, one new private helper, all load-bearing). The Opus review (run against the full phase diff before opening the PR) found real gaps in both: P01's fix armed its self-scheduled follow-up at a fixed 10ms delay borrowed from the unrelated ACK cadence, which silently turned a work-per-call bound into an undocumented ~3,200 pkt/s aggregate retransmit-rate ceiling (risking TLPKTDROP expiring a burst-loss tail before it regenerated) and could be perpetually postponed by frequent NAKs (latest-wins timer semantics) -- fixed to a zero-delay rearm, which bounds only what the card actually asks for. P02's status-override was unconditional (would have silently masked a future `Backpressured` status) and, more significantly, the review found admission.rs's `PeerTable` (the listener-side counterpart to caller.rs's `CallerTable`) has the identical unbounded due-pop pattern *and* no ready-drain budget mechanism at all -- assessed as needing new infrastructure comparable in size to this whole card, not a small addendum, and deliberately left as an explicit, tracked follow-up rather than a rushed partial fix to a different, heavily-tested shared module. Both P01's and P02's regression tests were also strengthened per review (exact sequence-identity assertions, not just counts). All fixes folded into their originating commits with new regressions proving each, not left as follow-up patches.
